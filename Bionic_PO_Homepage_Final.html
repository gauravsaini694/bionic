Basics

Prosthetic and orthotic services, explained simply

Prosthetics are artificial limbs that replace a missing arm, leg, hand, or foot. Orthotics are custom braces that support, align, and protect your body. Your doctor prescribes them, and a certified prosthetist or orthotist designs, fits, and adjusts them.

At Bionic, that work is done by certified, state-licensed clinicians who have cared for patients since 2007. Our team includes experienced practitioners such as Jeff Phelps, CPO, and Sheri Harper, CPO, and Bionic is ABC and BOC accredited.

We care for adults and children living with limb loss or limb difference, and anyone who needs a brace after injury, surgery, stroke, or a spinal condition.

From first visit to first steps

How getting a prosthesis or brace works

  1. Evaluation

    A certified clinician looks at your limb, strength, mobility, and goals. For a prosthetic leg, we also assess your K-level.

  2. Design and coverage

    We design your device, get your doctor's prescription, and help you through insurance approval.

  3. Fitting and testing

    We fit your device, watch how you move, and fine-tune it until it feels right.

  4. Training and follow-up

    Gait training helps you build confidence. After that, we see you whenever your body or goals change.

Know your mobility level

What is a K-level?

Medicare and most insurers rate how active you can be with a prosthetic leg on a scale from K0 to K4. Your K-level decides what your plan will cover, so we assess it carefully at your first visit.

K0Non-ambulatory

Can't walk or transfer safely, even with help. A prosthesis wouldn't improve mobility.

K1Household walker

Walks or transfers on level ground at a steady pace, mostly at home.

K2Limited community walker

Manages curbs, stairs, and uneven ground at a steady pace.

K3Community walker

Walks at different speeds and handles most obstacles, work, and exercise.

K4High activity

Goes beyond everyday walking, with high impact or stress. Typical of children, active adults, and athletes.

Not sure where you fit? Book a free evaluation and we'll work it out together.

Insurance and cost

What prosthetics and orthotics cost, and what insurance covers

Does Medicare cover prosthetics and braces?

Yes. Medicare Part B covers prosthetic limbs and medically necessary arm, leg, back, and neck braces your doctor orders. After the Part B deductible, you pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount.

How much does a prosthetic leg cost?

It depends on the amputation level, your K-level, and the components you need. Insurance usually covers most of it, so what you pay comes down to your deductible and coinsurance.

What's included?

Your first visit is free, and routine visits and adjustments are included in your device cost. Home and facility visits are also free.

Prosthetics and orthotics FAQ

Questions people ask before their first visit

What is the difference between a prosthetist and an orthotist?

A prosthetist designs and fits prostheses, which are artificial limbs that replace a missing arm, leg, hand, or foot. An orthotist designs and fits orthoses, which are braces that support, align, and protect the body.

Does Medicare cover prosthetics and orthotics?

Yes. Medicare Part B covers prosthetic limbs and medically necessary arm, leg, back, and neck braces when your doctor orders them. After the Part B deductible, you pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount.

How much does a prosthetic leg cost?

It depends on the amputation level, your K-level, and the components you need. Insurance usually covers most of it, so what you pay comes down to your deductible and coinsurance.

Do I need a prescription to see a prosthetist or orthotist?

Not to book a consultation. You will need a prescription from your doctor to receive a device, and we can help you get one.

Is the first visit free?

Yes. Your first visit at Bionic costs nothing.

What is a K-level?

A K-level is Medicare's rating, from K0 to K4, of how active you can be with a prosthetic leg. It decides which prosthetics insurance will cover.

Can a prosthetist or orthotist visit me at home?

Yes. If you can't get to a clinic, a Bionic specialist can see you at home, in a nursing facility, or in an assisted living facility at no charge.
