Prosthetic arms are artificial devices designed to replace missing upper limbs. Modern technology has advanced significantly from older designs, offering devices that mimic natural hand movements, respond to muscle signals, and even provide sensory feedback.

## Prevalence

According to a 2024 study, approximately 2.3 million Americans currently live with limb loss, with about 9% involving upper limbs. Common causes include workplace accidents, motor vehicle collisions, vascular disease, and congenital limb differences.

## Core Components

**Socket:** A custom-molded shell that fits over the residual limb, typically made from lightweight plastics, carbon fiber, or medical-grade silicone.

**Suspension System:** Keeps the prosthetic securely in place through harnesses, suction, or silicone liners.

**Wrist Unit:** Connects the terminal device to the arm, allowing for positioning and rotation. Some models feature quick-disconnect mechanisms for swapping attachments.

**Terminal Device:** Functions as the hand or hook, with options ranging from cosmetic hands to advanced myoelectric models with individually powered fingers.

## Control Systems

**Body-Powered:** Uses a cable-and-harness mechanism activated by shoulder and upper body movements, offering durability and proprioceptive feedback.

**Myoelectric:** Uses sensors that detect the electrical signals from muscles, providing a more natural appearance and finer motor control.

**Hybrid:** Combines both systems — for example, a body-powered elbow paired with a myoelectric hand.

## Modern Capabilities

Current technology offers multi-articulating hands with a range of grip patterns, pattern recognition systems, lightweight materials, custom cosmetic covers, and activity-specific attachments. That said, it's important to keep expectations realistic — prosthetics cannot fully replicate natural limb function, and they typically require weeks or months of occupational therapy to use well.
