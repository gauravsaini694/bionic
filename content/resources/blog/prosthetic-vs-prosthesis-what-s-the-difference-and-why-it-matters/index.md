Terminology around prosthetic devices often causes confusion, even among patients who have worn one for years. The core distinction is simple: "prosthesis" refers to the physical device itself, while "prosthetic" serves as a descriptive adjective. For example, a patient might say "I wear a prosthetic leg," using the adjective form in casual speech.

A prosthesis is the actual medical device that replaces a missing body part, and clinical documentation typically uses this term. Conversely, "prosthetic" describes items related to the device — such as a prosthetic socket, prosthetic care, or prosthetic fitting.

## Meet the Prosthetist

A prosthetist is a licensed healthcare professional who designs, fits, adjusts, and maintains prosthetic devices while providing ongoing patient support. This distinction matters because patients enter an ongoing care relationship rather than simply receiving a piece of equipment — your prosthetist is a long-term partner in your mobility and independence.

## Why the Terminology Doesn't Have to Be Perfect

Clear communication between patients and providers matters for better outcomes, but neither terminology choice is "wrong." Patients should not worry about using imprecise language when seeking care — your care team will understand what you mean.

## A Note on 3D Printing

3D printing is a fabrication method, not a device category. A device produced through 3D printing is still, functionally and clinically, a prosthesis — just built using a different manufacturing process than traditional methods.

Whether you call it a prosthetic or a prosthesis, what matters most is that it fits well, functions properly, and is supported by a care team you trust.
