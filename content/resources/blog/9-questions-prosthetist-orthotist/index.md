---
title: "9 Questions You Need to Ask Your Prosthetist or Orthotist"
date: 2022-07-05
category: "Prosthetics and Orthotics"
---

Appointments with your prosthetics or orthotics specialist offer real opportunities to enhance mobility, reduce pain, and improve overall quality of life. Getting the most out of these visits depends on asking informed questions and advocating for yourself. Here are nine essential questions to bring to your next appointment.

## 1. Experience with Your Condition

Ask about the clinician's specific experience treating patients with your type of amputation or your particular mobility needs. Expertise can vary quite a bit from case to case.

## 2. Advanced Technology Access

Find out whether the practice uses current technological innovations, like 3D printing or myoelectric limbs, that might be suited to your individual needs.

## 3. Support Group Connections

Ask whether your clinician can recommend local amputee support groups or national advocacy organizations that might be helpful.

## 4. Therapy Partnerships

Find out whether your provider has relationships with physical therapists who are experienced in limb loss and limb difference rehabilitation.

## 5. Device Options

Explore the prosthetic or orthotic styles, designs, and types available to you, along with the advantages and disadvantages of each option.

## 6. Device Problem Support

Clarify who to contact if your device malfunctions or needs repair, and how quickly you can expect to reach them.

## 7. Follow-Up Schedule

Establish a clear timeline for your next appointments so your device can be monitored and adjusted as needed.

## 8. Insurance Coverage

Confirm your provider's in-network status, any coverage limitations, and whether you'll be responsible for the difference between what your insurance pays and what the clinic charges.

## 9. Final Verification

Before you leave, ask whether you've covered everything necessary to make the most of your care.

## Key Takeaway

Patients get the most out of their care by actively participating in it — asking thorough questions and, ultimately, asking themselves: am I doing everything I can to advocate for my own care?
