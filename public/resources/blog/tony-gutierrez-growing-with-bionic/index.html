Tony Gutierrez: Growing with Bionic

Success Stories June 24, 2022
Back to Blog

Tony Gutierrez joined Bionic in 2014 at the Tinley Park, Illinois office after leaving a competitor focused primarily on revenue. He sought an organization that would foster his professional development with clear advancement pathways.

“I was given a yearly revenue goal with a plan to reach salary bonuses and an open path to reach it, but it was up to me to develop my referral sources,” he explains. Initial marketing challenges shifted once he recognized that patient advocacy and clinical team communication constituted effective practice-building.

Gutierrez expanded Bionic’s Chicago presence from one to five locations and pioneered entry into remote markets, establishing thriving practices in Lafayette, Terre Haute, and Indianapolis. Success stemmed from integrating clinical teams into medical communities while maintaining quality patient-driven outcome goals and impeccable clinical and follow-up care.

Community Education & Empowerment

Prior to prosthetics and orthotics, Gutierrez worked as an educator — a focus he maintained professionally. Bionic encouraged expanded community involvement, leading to a partnership with a local Indiana medical magnet high school offering day-long seminars introducing the prosthetics and orthotics field. Program graduates have gone on to become clinic interns.

Commitment to Research

In 2019, Gutierrez collaborated with Rosalind Franklin University and the University of Michigan on a Department of Defense prosthetic research study examining how adjustable above-knee socket solutions affect patient preference, comfort, mobility, and balance. The initiative, roughly halfway through recruitment at the time (targeting 15 participants), was expected to generate publications advancing socket design understanding.

This partnership expanded to include multiple projects: a strength comparison of 3D-printed versus standard sockets, subischial socket clinical effectiveness surveys, and subischial socket evaluation in K2-level populations. Bionic additionally partnered with Valparaiso University investigating Reciprocating Gait Orthosis design variations for pediatric applications.

Opportunities for Outreach Around the World

Spring 2019 brought an opportunity to assist one of Bionic’s clinics in India. Partnering with a rehabilitation hospital in Ghaziabad, Gutierrez and colleagues evaluated, treated, and designed prosthetic solutions for more than 30 underserved patients in four days. Many received first-time or replacement prostheses, gaining increased mobility and bilateral hand function.

“This was truly a life changing experience,” Gutierrez reflects, noting ongoing international professional relationships developed through this work.

Unlimited Opportunity, Key Support

Bionic provided freedom to develop a customized career path matching his strengths. In 2022, Gutierrez transitioned to National Clinical Specialist for Advanced Prosthetics Solutions — a role created through his pursuit of optimal technology applications, educational commitment, and willingness to assist colleagues with complex cases.

“Bionic has presented itself to me as an open canvas where I can set my own course and change it along the way,” he concludes, emphasizing that designing one’s practice to leverage individual strengths represents the most valuable professional opportunity available.
